Finding 3

Cash vs. Stock

Cash deals show the pre-announcement run-up; a direct pooled test supports a larger effect than stock.
The evidence supports concentration in cash, not proof of a cash-only effect.

1  Descriptive side-by-side
Separate complete-case panels; baseline is e ≤ -3 plus never-acquirers. Descriptive only.
2  Formal pooled test
Cash and stock indicators enter the same regression.

Concentrated in cash
The direct Wald difference, not two separate significance results, is the test.
Supported, but fragile: stock is imprecisely negative. The thesis therefore says concentrated, not strictly cash-specific.
Pooled sample
25,600 firm-quarters
1,191 firms
Sample and baseline
Pre-deal quarters before the first ≥50% cash or stock acquisition, plus firms making neither. Neither-deal firms are the baseline.
Controls and fixed effects
Leverage, ln(assets), Tobin's Q, ROA, capex, dividend indicator, cash-flow volatility; firm and calendar year-quarter fixed effects.
Inference
Firm-clustered SEs; two-tailed tests. Within-firm, descriptive; not causal.
Thesis source: Main Analysis 3; Tables “Event Study by Payment Type: Cash versus Stock Acquirers” and “Cash-Specificity Test: Cash versus Stock Acquirers.” Approximate 95% CIs derived from reported SEs.
