# Claim-Strength Audit Exceptions

## CLM-001 | Major | Slide 5 | OVERSTATED

**Deck wording**

> No prior work occupies this exact cell.

**Controlling thesis sentence**

`_intro_body.tex`:

> To our knowledge no prior work reads uncertainty language in the unscripted question-and-answer session in the anticipatory window before a withheld deal; we offer this as a positioning claim about where the contribution sits, not as a tested mechanism.

**Difference in strength**

The deck removes the epistemic hedge "To our knowledge" and turns a bounded positioning claim into a categorical claim about the entire literature.

**What a listener could wrongly conclude**

A listener could conclude that the thesis has established exhaustively that no prior study occupies the cell, rather than reporting the author's literature-based positioning judgment.

## CLM-002 | Major | Slide 13 | OVERSTATED

**Deck wording**

> Unscripted CEO Q&A carries a readable, anticipatory trace  
> of a deal's passage from private to public.

**Controlling thesis sentence**

`_conclusion_body.tex`:

> Taken together, these patterns suggest that the unscripted language of earnings calls carries a readable, anticipatory trace of a deal's passage from private to public, a signature researchers can in principle observe before the announcement.

**Difference in strength**

The final slide removes "these patterns suggest that" and presents the interpretive conclusion as an established property of CEO Q&A. Slide 11 uses the weaker interpretive construction "Reads unscripted Q&A as tracking a deal's passage from private to public," so the final slide also states the recurring claim more strongly than the contribution slide.

**What a listener could wrongly conclude**

A listener could conclude that the design directly establishes a readable trace, rather than that the descriptive patterns support that interpretation.

## CLM-003 | Minor | Slide 13 | OVERSTATED

**Deck wording**

> The pre-announcement run-up is  
> stronger in cash deals than in stock  
> deals.

**Controlling thesis sentences**

`sec34_body_from_ledgers(1).tex`:

> This analysis tests hypothesis~H1a: that the pre-announcement uncertainty run-up is stronger for cash acquirers than for stock acquirers. We find H1a supported but fragile: one formal cash-minus-stock test clears the five-percent level, but its significance rides on the imprecise stock estimate, so we read the result as concentration in cash deals rather than strict specificity.

**Difference in strength**

The final recap repeats the between-group conclusion without its load-bearing qualifications that the result is fragile and rests on the formal cash-minus-stock test. Slide 10 carries both qualifications, but slide 13 states the claim flat.

**What a listener could wrongly conclude**

A listener could treat the cash-versus-stock difference as a stable, unqualified comparison or infer it from the separate arm estimates rather than from the formal pooled contrast.

## CLM-004 | Minor | Slide 13 | MISMATCH

**Deck wording**

> The uncertainty signal  
> fades.

**Controlling thesis sentence**

`_conclusion_body.tex`:

> We find that residual uncertainty is elevated in the quarter before a cash acquisition, with no comparable rise before stock acquisitions; that it becomes indistinguishable from zero once the deal is announced, even before it closes, while the cash that funds it persists to completion; and that the elevation concentrates in cash acquisitions rather than stock on a formal pooled test.

**Difference in strength**

"Fades" weakens the thesis's stronger timing claim that residual uncertainty becomes indistinguishable from zero at announcement. It also differs from slide 9, which says it "becomes indistinguishable from baseline at announcement."

**What a listener could wrongly conclude**

A listener could conclude that uncertainty merely declines after announcement rather than returning to the estimated baseline.

# Required Questions

**q1. Does any slide assert or imply causation, and if so, in what exact words?**  
None after accounting for the deck's explicit descriptive and noncausal qualifiers.

**q2. Does any slide present a mechanism as established rather than as motivation or an open question?**  
None. Slide 2 leaves compliance-constrained versus strategically chosen silence unresolved, and the deck does not establish a war-chest or cash-accumulation mechanism.

**q3. Does any slide read the payment-method result as stricter than concentration in cash, or treat the imprecise stock estimate as evidence of absence?**  
None. Slide 10 explicitly says "concentration in cash, not proof of a cash-only effect" and says the stock estimate is imprecise and is not read as a decline.

**q4. Is every between-group and between-stage claim tied to the formal contrast built to test it?**  
No. Slide 13 repeats the cash-versus-stock claim without the formal pooled-test basis or fragility qualification. See CLM-003. The between-stage recap also uses the weaker "fades" wording rather than the formally supported return-to-baseline claim. See CLM-004.

**q5. Where does the deck drop a hedge that the thesis uses for the same claim?**  
Slide 5 drops "To our knowledge." Slide 13 drops "these patterns suggest that" and omits "supported but fragile" from the cash-versus-stock recap. See CLM-001 through CLM-003.

**q6. Do the four boundaries stated on slide 12 actually cover the claims the rest of the deck makes, and is any boundary stated more narrowly than the thesis states it?**  
No exception. Read with the explicit notes on slides 2, 7, 10, and 11, the boundaries cover causal identification, mechanism, observational equivalence, measurement, generalizability, strict cash-specificity, and the imperfect stock comparison.

**q7. Is any claim asserted at one strength on one slide and a different strength on another?**  
Yes. Slide 13's flat "carries" is stronger than slide 11's interpretive "Reads ... as tracking" and the thesis's "patterns suggest." Slide 13's "fades" is weaker than slide 9's "becomes indistinguishable from baseline." Slide 13 also omits the fragility stated on slide 10 for the cash-versus-stock result. See CLM-002 through CLM-004.
