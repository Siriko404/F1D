1. THE BIG PICTURE
================================================================================

1.1 The presentation is not a chapter-by-chapter thesis summary
----------------------------------------------------------------

The defense should tell one results-forward story:

  A firm privately commits to a potentially material acquisition.
  It may remain silent about the deal, but it cannot mislead if it speaks.
  A scheduled earnings call still occurs, and analysts ask questions in Q&A.
  The thesis asks whether this private information state leaves a trace in the
  CEO's unscripted language, and whether that trace changes when the deal
  becomes public.

The central verbal question is:

  Does withholding leave a trace in the CEO's language?

The central empirical answer is:

  CEO residual uncertainty rises within firm in the quarter before a cash
  acquisition. It is indistinguishable from zero after announcement but before
  completion. The run-up concentrates in cash deals relative to stock deals.

This remains a correlational descriptive result. The thesis does not identify
a causal mechanism.


1.2 The primitive setting
-------------------------

Use this safe formulation:

  - The firm knows about a pending acquisition.
  - The pending deal may be material.
  - The firm may stay silent.
  - If the firm speaks, it cannot mislead.
  - A routine or scheduled earnings call still occurs.
  - Analysts ask questions in unscripted Q&A.

Do NOT say:

  "The CEO is legally required to host an earnings call."

That statement is not needed and is unsafe. The presentation only needs the
observational fact that routine calls continue during the private period.


1.3 What the thesis adds
------------------------

Prior work has studied signals such as:

  - Market prices.
  - Managed tone.
  - Strategy or deal-specific vocabulary.

This thesis studies something different:

  Residual uncertainty in the CEO's unscripted Q&A answers before a private
  acquisition becomes public.

The contribution is best stated as:

  A within-firm language signature aligned with the private-to-public boundary.

Do not call it a causal effect, a proven mechanism, a trading strategy, or a
general detector of all hidden corporate events.

SLIDE 4 - SEPARATING SPEAKING STYLE FROM CALL-SPECIFIC UNCERTAINTY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Title:
  Separating speaking style from call-specific uncertainty

Main decomposition on screen:

  Observed CEO Q&A uncertainty
      = CEO style
      + predictable call factors
      + UncResCEO

Three-stage pipeline:

  1. START WITH LANGUAGE
     Raw uncertainty share

     Loughran-McDonald uncertainty words divided by total words in the CEO's
     Q&A answers.

  2. REMOVE WHAT IS PREDICTABLE
     Style and call conditions

     - CEO fixed effects
     - Prepared-remarks uncertainty (UncPreCEO)
     - Analyst-question uncertainty (UncQue)
     - Call negativity (NegCall)
     - Firm performance controls
     - Calendar year

  3. KEEP THE RESIDUAL
     UncResCEO

     The call-specific component of CEO uncertainty, net of persistent style
     and observable call conditions.

Bottom guardrails:
  - Q&A only: unscripted CEO answers
  - At least five calls required for each CEO
  - State, not personality: interpreted within CEO over time

Purpose:
  Make the dependent variable intuitive before showing data or regressions.

Suggested spoken content:

  "I begin with the share of Loughran-McDonald uncertainty words in the CEO's
  Q&A answers. Raw levels are not enough because executives differ persistently
  in how tentatively they speak. I therefore residualize the raw share using CEO
  fixed effects and observable call conditions. The residual, UncResCEO, is the
  call-specific component used in the main tests. A CEO needs at least five calls
  for the measure to be constructed."

Important statistical caveat for questions or backup:

  UncResCEO is a generated regressand. Conventional second-stage standard errors
  may be understated unless uncertainty from the first stage is carried through,
  for example through a two-step correction or bootstrap. Do not dismiss this as
  merely adding harmless measurement noise.

Audience takeaway:
  The dependent variable is not the CEO's general personality or average
  tentativeness. It captures what is unusual about that CEO on that call.

Transition:

  "I construct this measure for a large panel of earnings calls and link those
  calls to acquisition events."

Timing:
  Approximately 1:35.

  PRE1  Quarter immediately before announcement

  1. RUN-UP

  - Calendar year-quarter fixed effects

  - Standard errors clustered by firm

SLIDE 12 - WHAT THE THESIS ESTABLISHES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Title:
  What the thesis establishes

Three established findings:

  1. Within firm, CEO residual uncertainty rises in the quarter before a cash
     acquisition.

  2. The language pattern is concentrated in the private period and is
     indistinguishable from zero after announcement but before completion.

  3. The pre-announcement run-up is significantly stronger for cash than stock
     deals in a formal pooled comparison.

What the thesis does not establish:

  - Causal identification
  - The psychological reason the CEO uses more uncertain language
  - A unique or exclusive cash-deal mechanism
  - A profitable trading strategy
  - Generalization beyond the observed sample and setting

Core interpretive boundary:
  Compliance-constrained speech and strategic silence may be observationally
  equivalent in these data. The thesis cannot distinguish them cleanly.

Preferred final sentence:

  "A withheld acquisition leaves a readable, anticipatory trace in the CEO's
  unscripted answers."

Suggested spoken close:

  "The thesis establishes a within-firm pre-announcement language run-up, a
  sharp change at the private-to-public boundary, and a formal concentration in
  cash deals relative to stock deals. It does not identify why the CEO's
  language changes or claim a causal mechanism. The main takeaway is that a
  withheld acquisition leaves a readable, anticipatory trace in the CEO's
  unscripted answers."

Audience takeaway:
  The result is interesting because of its timing and within-firm structure, and

7. ANTICIPATED QUESTIONS AND SHORT ANSWER LOGIC
================================================================================

Q1. Why cash deals?

Short answer:
  The main evidence indicates that the pre-announcement language run-up is
  concentrated in cash deals. Cash and stock transactions create different
  financing and disclosure incentives, so stock is a useful managed comparison,
  not a perfect counterfactual. The thesis does not identify payment method as a
  causal mechanism.


Q2. Why is stock not a placebo?

Short answer:
  Stock deals can have their own information and currency-protection incentives.
  The comparison tests whether the run-up is stronger in cash deals, not whether
  stock firms are untreated in every relevant dimension.


Q3. Why use a dictionary rather than machine learning?

Short answer:
  The Loughran-McDonald lexicon is transparent, reproducible, finance-specific,
  and interpretable. The thesis question concerns a stable uncertainty construct,
  and residualization addresses persistent speaker style. A supervised model
  could improve prediction but would introduce training-label and interpretability
  choices outside this thesis's design.


Q4. How do you know the words belong to the CEO?

Short answer:
  Capital IQ transcripts identify speakers, and Execucomp is used to identify the
  CEO. The measure uses the CEO's Q&A answers, not the full transcript.


Q5. Why require at least five calls?

Short answer:
  The residualization needs repeated observations to distinguish persistent CEO
  style from call-specific variation. The tradeoff is sample selection toward
  larger, better-covered firms.


Q6. Is UncResCEO a generated dependent variable?

Short answer:
  Yes. It is estimated in a first stage and then used in the event regressions.
  Conventional second-stage standard errors may understate total uncertainty if
  first-stage estimation error is ignored. A bootstrap or explicit two-step
  correction would be a useful extension.


Q7. Is the result causal?

Short answer:
  No. Firm fixed effects and event timing remove important stable differences and
  organize the correlation, but no exogenous assignment or instrument identifies
  causality. The thesis establishes a within-firm timing regularity.


Q8. Could analysts simply ask more cash-related questions?

Short answer:
  The pre-announcement coefficient remains approximately 0.041 after incorporating
  measured cash scrutiny, and the interaction is insignificant. The correct
  conclusion is that measured scrutiny does not account for the run-up. The test
  is underpowered, so scrutiny is not ruled out generally.


Q9. Could ordinary firm uncertainty drive the result?

Short answer:
  The residualization removes prepared-remarks uncertainty, question uncertainty,
  call negativity, firm performance, CEO style, and time effects. Firm and
  calendar fixed effects are also included. Remaining time-varying omitted states
  are still possible, which is why the result is correlational.


Q10. Why is announcement more important than completion?

Short answer:
  Announcement changes the information state: the deal becomes public. Completion
  changes the transaction state: payment occurs and cash is depleted. Language
  aligns with the first boundary, while cash aligns with the second.


Q11. What does the negative POST uncertainty coefficient mean?

Short answer:
  It is marginal at the 10 percent level and is not a central result. The robust
  claim is that PRE1 is elevated and GAP is indistinguishable from zero, with a
  significant PRE1-GAP contrast.


Q12. Does the result generalize to small firms?

Short answer:
  The sample requires repeated calls and Execucomp CEO coverage, so it is tilted
  toward larger, better-covered firms. Generalization to small or sparsely covered
  firms is an open question.


Q13. Does the language measure predict returns or spreads?

Short answer:
  The thesis's market-consequence evidence is secondary and limited. UncResCEO is
  not robustly related to bid-ask spreads in the secondary analysis. The core
  contribution is the event-time language regularity, not a market-trading claim.


Q14. What is the most important result?

Short answer:
  The event-time result: residual CEO uncertainty is elevated in the last private
  quarter, but is indistinguishable from zero once the deal is public even before
  completion. That timing separates the information boundary from closing.


Q15. What would you do next?

Short answer:
  Improve first-stage uncertainty propagation, test alternative language models,
  broaden the firm universe, study additional private corporate events, and seek
  designs that better separate strategic silence, compliance constraints, and
  general managerial uncertainty.
