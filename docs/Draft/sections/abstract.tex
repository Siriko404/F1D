\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We test whether speech uncertainty conveyed by chief executive officers during quarterly earnings calls predicts firm cash holdings, using a sample of $112{,}968$ earnings-call transcripts covering $2{,}429$ U.S.\ firms over fiscal years 2002--2018. We adopt the three-component decomposition of \citeA{dzielinski2021}: a persistent CEO-style trait (\texttt{ClarityCEO}, the negative of a CEO fixed effect estimated from prepared-remarks segments), a call-level state residual within the Q\&A segment (\texttt{UncResCEO}), and a presentation-segment uncertainty share (\texttt{UncPreCEO}). The state residual---which \citeauthor{dzielinski2021} characterize as ``explaining little'' of the market price reaction---loads positively on cash holdings in all twelve specifications of a four-step fixed-effects panel ($p<0.10$ one-tailed; eight of twelve at $p<0.05$), and persistent CEO style loads negatively in nine of twelve. Same variable, different outcome class, opposite verdict: the Q\&A residual that does not move stock prices does move corporate cash decisions. The state-residual response amplifies among credit-constrained Unrated firms on the one-quarter-lead dependent variable (two of eight cells at $p<0.05$ in the \citeA{faulkender2006} interaction), and among firms in the upper half of the cash-flow-volatility distribution \cite{hanqiu2007} at one-tier-weaker significance. Construct-validation regressions show CEO speech uncertainty itself responds positively to firm-level political risk \cite{hassan2020}, US and global news-based economic-policy-uncertainty indices \cite{baker2016,davis2016}, and product-market competition \cite{hobergphillips2016}. Outside-world reaction regressions show that the post-call $26$-day quoted bid-ask spread and SEC comment-letter receipt both load \emph{presentation-segment} uncertainty---the segment \citeauthor{dzielinski2021} characterize as scripted firm-culture-reflecting---rather than the Q\&A components. Three identification designs (CEO sudden-death difference-in-differences, \citeauthor{dzielinski2021} \S6 first-difference replication around CEO turnover, and \citeA{lewbel2012} heteroskedasticity-based instrumental-variable estimation) address reverse-causality, time-invariant heterogeneity, and time-varying omitted-variable threats respectively.
\end{abstract}
