● Let me think through this carefully before writing any code.

  Pecking Order Theory and the Proposed DV

  The theory (Myers & Majluf, 1984): Firms prefer internal financing (retained
  earnings, cash flow) first, then debt, then equity last. The driver is
  information asymmetry -- external financing is costly because outsiders know
  less about firm value.

  The proposed DV: Binary = 1 if external funding > internal funding, 0
  otherwise.

  Construction would likely be:
  - Internal funds = operating cash flow (oancf) or net income + depreciation
  (ib + dp)
  - External funds = net debt issuance (dltis - dltr) + net equity issuance
  (sstk - prstkc)
  - Binary = 1 if External > Internal

  Does it make sense? My honest assessment: probably not.

  Problem 1: Tautological overlap with existing DVs. The binary DV is
  mechanically composed of the same balance sheet items that are already DVs in
  other suites:
  - Cash holdings (H1) = accumulated internal funds
  - Leverage (H4a/b) = external debt usage
  - Repurchases (H17) = returning cash instead of retaining it internally
  - Payout (H12) = same

  Finding that uncertainty predicts External_Funding_Majority would not be a new
   finding -- it would just bundle H1 + H4 + H17 into a single variable. We
  already know the answer: uncertainty pushes firms toward internal funding
  (more cash, less debt, fewer payouts).

  Problem 2: It doesn't "explain" the other findings -- it IS the other 
  findings. Your supervisor suggested it might explain cash, capex, R&D. But for
   it to explain those, it would need to be a mediator or an independent
  variable, not another DV in the same regression framework. As a DV predicted
  by the same IVs (speech uncertainty), it just adds another row to the findings
   table without resolving any of the open questions.

  Problem 3: The existing results already tell the pecking order story. Look at
  your Manager_QA findings read together:
  - Cash ↑ (H1): building internal reserves
  - Leverage ↓ (H4a/b): avoiding external debt
  - Payout ↓ (H12, via Pres): retaining cash
  - Repurchases ↓ (H17): not disbursing cash

  This IS the pecking order prediction. A binary DV just re-packages what's
  already there.

  Problem 4: Binary DV construction issues. Many firms have negative operating
  cash flow in a given quarter (loss-making firms), making the internal/external
   split ambiguous. The >50% threshold is arbitrary. And if most firm-quarters
  are internally funded (which is empirically true), you get a skewed base rate
  similar to H18's CCCL problem (0.27% base rate, near-zero R-squared).

  When would it make sense?

  It would make sense as a mediation test: Uncertainty → funding choice shift →
  cash/capex/R&D. But that requires a different methodology (Sobel test,
  structural equations, or at minimum a two-stage design), not the standard
  PanelOLS we use. Adding it as another standalone DV suite gives you a 13th
  result that says the same thing as the other 12.

  What would actually help explain the findings?

  The real open questions from the findings are:
  - Q40/Q52: Why do Manager_QA and Manager_Pres have opposite signs in H17?
  - Q9/Q50: Why do CEO_QA and Manager_QA have opposite signs in H4a/H16?
  - Q30: Why does TSIMM moderate capex (H13.1) but not cash (H1.1)?
  - Q56-57: How can uncertainty increase BOTH cash AND capex simultaneously?

  A funding-source binary DV doesn't address any of these.

  My recommendation: Don't implement this suite. The pecking order story is
  already embedded in your existing results. If your supervisor wants a pecking
  order angle, the better approach is to frame the existing H1 + H4 + H17
  results together as pecking order evidence in the paper's discussion section,
  rather than creating a new DV that mechanically recombines them.

  direction?
